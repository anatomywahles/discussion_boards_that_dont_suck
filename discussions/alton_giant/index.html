Discussion Board Instructions

Part 1 - Learn About Gigantism & Robert Wadlow

In this discussion, you will explore the impact of pituitary gigantism on the life of Robert Wadlow.

By participating in this discussion, you will gain a better understanding of the impact of growth hormone (GH) on the human body.

Learn About Gigantism

Start by reading this article from Cleveland Clinic to learn some general information about pituitary gigantism.

As you're reading, take notes about these kinds of things (to help you understand gigantism):

  • What organs & hormones are involved in the development of gigantism?
  • What are the typical symptoms of gigantism?
  • What treatment options are available for gigantism?

Learn About Robert Wadlow

Once you have a general understanding of gigantism, watch this video about Robert Wadlow, a patient with gigantism.

As you're watching the video, take notes about these kinds of things (to help you with your discussion post):

  • When did Robert start showing signs he had gigantism?
  • How did Robert's gigantism impact his day-to-day life?
  • What special opportunities did Robert's gigantism bring him?
  • What lead to Robert's death?
Part 2 - Your Discussion Board Post

Start your post by listing three specific details from the video that stuck out to you.

  • Specific details are NOT things like, "Robert had gigantism" or "Robert was very tall."
  • Specific details ARE things like, "Robert was born in 1918". (Sorry... since I used this one, you need to find at least three others!)

Then, answer one of these questions:

Option #1

Robert's gigantism resulted from excessive production of growth hormone (GH) by his pituitary gland. How did that disruption of the hypothalamic-pituitary-somatotropic (HPS) axis contribute to the challenges he faced?

Option #2

Robert often attracted public attention because of his height. How does his story influence your understanding of the real-world impacts of endocrine disorders on a person's everyday life?

Option #3

If treatments had existed during Robert's childhood that could have limited his growth, should doctors and parents have used them? Explain your reasoning.

Please note: there are NO right or wrong answers for the questions listed above!

  • The goal of these questions is for you to connect Robert's story to the physiology of the endocrine system and the "human side" of medicine (i.e. the ethics, emotions, etc.).
  • As long as you write a thoughtful reply, you WILL receive full credit!
    • A thoughtful reply provides specific ideas to support your answer.
    • A thoughtful reply could also address the potential concerns of someone who might not agree with your answer.
