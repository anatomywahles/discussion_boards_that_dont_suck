Discussion Board Instructions

Part 1: Learn About Gigantism & Robert Wadlow

In this discussion, you will explore the impact of pituitary gigantism on the life of Robert Wadlow. By participating in this discussion, you will gain a better understanding of the role of growth hormone (GH) in the human body.

Step 1: Learn About Gigantism

Start by reading the Cleveland Clinic article about pituitary gigantism.

As you read, take notes about:

  • What organs and hormones are involved in the development of gigantism?
  • What are the typical symptoms of gigantism?
  • What treatment options are available for gigantism?

Step 2: Learn About Robert Wadlow

Once you have a general understanding of gigantism, watch the video about Robert Wadlow.

As you watch, take notes about:

  • When Robert first showed signs of gigantism.
  • How gigantism affected his day-to-day life.
  • What special opportunities his condition created.
  • What ultimately led to his death.

Part 2: Your Discussion Post

Start your post by listing three specific details from the video that stood out to you.

Not specific enough:

  • Robert had gigantism.
  • Robert was very tall.

Specific details include things like:

  • Robert was born in 1918.
  • A specific challenge he experienced.
  • A unique aspect of his daily life.

Then answer ONE of the following questions:

Option 1

Robert's gigantism resulted from excessive production of growth hormone (GH) by his pituitary gland. How did disruption of the hypothalamic-pituitary-somatotropic (HPS) axis contribute to the challenges he faced?

Option 2

Robert often attracted public attention because of his height. How does his story influence your understanding of the real-world impacts of endocrine disorders on a person's everyday life?

Option 3

If treatments had existed during Robert's childhood that could have limited his growth, should doctors and parents have used them? Explain your reasoning.

Important: There are no right or wrong answers to the questions above.
  • The goal is to connect Robert's story to endocrine physiology and the human side of medicine.
  • Thoughtful responses earn full credit.
  • Support your ideas with specific evidence and reasoning.
  • Consider alternative viewpoints when appropriate.

Part 3: Peer Replies

After submitting your original post, reply to at least two classmates.

Both replies should provide meaningful reflection on your peer's response.

Avoid:

  • "I agree."
  • "I don't think that's right."

Instead, consider starting with:

  • "I thought it was interesting how you discussed..."
  • "That relates to something I mentioned about..."
  • "After reading your post, I still wonder..."

Imagine you are having a genuine conversation with your classmate.
